ACS offers three approaches to cite sources in-text: Superscript, Italic Numbers 
in Parentheses, and Author-Date.  
Superscript and Italic Numbers in Parentheses are the generally preferred 
methods because they distract less from the paper's content and consume less space 
on the page.  
Author-Date is used when the year the article was published is important, such as 
in a literature review.

In the ACS (American Chemical Society) style, when using superscript numbers for in-text 
citations (e.g., ¹, ², ³), the references at the end of the document are organized numerically, 
corresponding to the order of their first appearance in the text. 
In-Text Citations: 
 
Use superscript numbers for in-text citations without parentheses or brackets. 
Example:  
The reaction was studied extensively.¹ Later experiments confirmed these findings.2,3 
The largest lesion in the first study was 10 cm.13 
The theory was first put forward in 1987.1 
Scholtz2 has argued that... 
Several recent studies3,4,15,16 have suggested that... 
For example, see7. 
Reference List Format: 
 
List the references in numerical order, matching the order of their superscript 
appearance in the text. 
 
Each reference starts with its number (not superscripted), followed by a period. 
 
Format the references according to the ACS guidelines for the specific source type (e.g., 
journal articles, books).

In the ACS (American Chemical Society) style, if you use the author-date citation format in 
the text (e.g., Smith, 2023), the references at the end of the document are presented in 
alphabetical order by the authors' last names. Here's how to format them: 
In-Text Citations: 
 
Use the author's last name and the year in parentheses. 
Example: The reaction was studied extensively (Smith, 2023). Later experiments 
confirmed these findings (Doe, 2022; Brown and Green, 2021). 
Reference List Format: 
 
Arrange the references alphabetically by author’s last name. 
 
If multiple works by the same author are cited, list them chronologically. 
 
Follow the standard ACS format for each type of source (journal articles, books, etc.).

Same First Author, Multiple Sources 
If the author of a paper is first author of multiple sources, list single-author works first, 
then two-author works, then multiple-author works. Within the groups of same-first-author 
works cited (e.g., single, dual, multiple), list the works chronologically. Add a lowercase letter 
for identification if the works cited were published in the same year. 
 
For example: 
Hamilton, F. J. Article Title. Biochemistry 2003, 42, 78-86. DOI. 
Hamilton F. J. Article Title. J. Agric. Food Chem. 2004a, 52, 1622-1633. DOI. 
Hamilton F. J. Article Title. J. Org Chem. 2004b, 69, 298-306. DOI. 
Hamilton, F. J.; Salvo, P. A. Article Title. J. Agric. Food Chem. 2005, 53, 918-924. DOI.

Chicago-style source citations come in two varieties:  
Author-date: The author-date system is more common in the sciences and social 
sciences.  
Notes and bibliography: The notes and bibliography system is preferred by many 
working in the humanities—including literature, history, and the arts.

Notes and bibliography 
This system uses footnotes or endnotes for in-text citations and a bibliography at the end. 
 Each time a source is used in the text, it must be cited by note: footnote or endnote. 
• 
 Footnotes appear at the foot (bottom) of the page and are preferred. 
• 
 Endnotes appear at the end of the paper before the bibliography.  
(Endnotes are useful when footnotes have become exorbitant.) 
 
Include a superscript number at the end of the sentence. 
Example:         Scholars argue that this concept is significant.¹ 
 
In the footnote or endnote: 
¹ Author's First and Last Name, Title of the Book (Place of Publication: Publisher, Year), 
Page Number. 
Example: 
¹ John Smith, Understanding History (New York: Oxford University Press, 2020), 45. 
Subsequent references: 
Use a shortened version: 
² Smith, Understanding History, 45. 
Journal article 
In a note, cite specific page numbers. In the bibliography, include the page range for the whole 
article. For articles consulted online, include a URL or the name of the database. Many journal 
articles list a DOI (Digital Object Identifier). A DOI forms a permanent URL that begins 
https://doi.org/. This URL is preferable to the URL that appears in your browser’s address bar.
